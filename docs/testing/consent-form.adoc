== About This Study

=== What is the purpose of the research?

This study is part of a PhD research project investigating infrastructure for
pragmatic epistemology in investigative journalism. The Evidence Graph system
combines three concepts:

. *PROMPT scoring*: A six-dimension framework for evaluating evidence quality
  (Provenance, Replicability, Objective, Methodology, Publication, Transparency).
. *Boundary objects*: The same evidence can be viewed from different stakeholder
  perspectives (researcher, policymaker, journalist, etc.).
. *i-docs navigation*: Interactive, non-linear navigation through evidence,
  where readers choose their own path.

The purpose of this user testing session is to evaluate whether these concepts
are usable and useful for working investigative journalists.

=== Why have I been invited?

You have been invited because you are a member of the National Union of
Journalists (NUJ) with professional journalism experience. We are seeking
feedback from 25 practising journalists to evaluate the system's usability and
relevance to investigative journalism practice.

=== What will I be asked to do?

If you agree to participate, you will be asked to:

. Complete a series of tasks using the Evidence Graph prototype system (approximately 60 minutes).
. Complete a written questionnaire about your experience (approximately 15 minutes).
. Participate in a brief debrief conversation about your impressions (approximately 20 minutes).

The total session duration is approximately 2 hours.

=== Will I be recorded?

With your explicit consent (see Section 6 below), the following may be recorded:

* *Screen recording*: A recording of your screen while using the system, to
  capture how you interact with the interface.
* *Audio recording*: A recording of your spoken comments during the think-aloud
  tasks and the debrief conversation.

You may participate without being recorded. If you consent to recording, you may
request that recording be paused or stopped at any time.

== Voluntary Participation and Right to Withdraw

Your participation in this study is entirely voluntary. You are free to:

* Decline to participate without giving a reason.
* Withdraw from the study at any time during the session without giving a reason.
* Request that your data be withdrawn after the session, up until the point of
  anonymisation and aggregation (see Section 4 for the withdrawal deadline).

If you withdraw, any data collected from you will be deleted and will not be
included in the analysis.

*Withdrawal deadline*: You may request withdrawal of your data up to 14 days
after your testing session. After this period, your data will have been
anonymised and aggregated, and it will no longer be possible to identify and
remove your individual contribution.

To withdraw, contact: j.d.a.jewell@open.ac.uk, quoting your participant
identifier (provided to you at the start of the session).

== Data Handling and Privacy

=== What data will be collected?

[cols="1,2,1"]
|===
| Data Type | Description | Identifiable?

| *Task performance data*
| Task completion rates, time on task, errors, hints used
| No (recorded by participant ID only)

| *Questionnaire responses*
| Likert-scale ratings and open-ended text responses
| No (recorded by participant ID only)

| *Screen recordings*
| Video of screen during task completion
| Potentially (if personal information visible on screen)

| *Audio recordings*
| Voice recordings during think-aloud and debrief
| Yes (voice is identifiable)

| *Observation notes*
| Facilitator's notes on participant behaviour
| No (recorded by participant ID only)

| *Demographic data (optional)*
| Career stage, outlet type, specialism, technical confidence
| No (aggregated only)
|===

=== How will my data be stored?

* All digital data will be stored on encrypted storage (AES-256) on institutional
  infrastructure or approved cloud services within the European Economic Area
  (EEA).
* Participant IDs will be stored separately from any identifying information
  (name, email). The link between IDs and identities will be held in a separate
  encrypted file accessible only to the principal investigator.
* Screen and audio recordings will be stored on encrypted local storage and will
  not be uploaded to cloud services.

=== How will my data be used?

* Data will be analysed in aggregate to evaluate the usability of the Evidence
  Graph system.
* Anonymised quotations from open-ended responses and debrief conversations may
  be included in the PhD thesis and related academic publications.
* No individual participant will be identifiable in any published output.
* Data will not be shared with third parties outside the research team (principal
  investigator and PhD supervisors).

=== How long will my data be retained?

|===
| Data Type | Retention Period

| Deleted within 6 months of the testing session, or upon completion of analysis, whichever is sooner

| *Anonymised questionnaire responses*
| Retained for the duration of the PhD programme (up to 5 years) and archived per Open University data retention policy

=== Legal basis for processing (GDPR)

The legal basis for processing your personal data under the UK General Data
Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) is:

* *Article 6(1)(a)*: Consent -- you have given clear consent for the processing
  of your personal data for the specific purpose of this research.
* *Article 9(2)(j)*: Processing is necessary for archiving purposes in the
  public interest, scientific or historical research purposes, or statistical
  purposes (for anonymised data retained after the withdrawal deadline).

=== Your rights under UK GDPR

You have the right to:

* Access your personal data held by the researcher.
* Rectify inaccurate personal data.
* Request erasure of your personal data (subject to the withdrawal deadline above).
* Restrict or object to the processing of your personal data.
* Lodge a complaint with the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) at
  https://ico.org.uk/ if you believe your data has been mishandled.

To exercise any of these rights, contact: j.d.a.jewell@open.ac.uk

== Risks and Benefits

=== Potential risks

The risks of participating in this study are minimal. You may experience:

* Mild frustration if the prototype system is difficult to use (this is expected
  and is precisely what we are testing).
* Time commitment of approximately 2 hours.

There are no physical, psychological, or professional risks associated with
participation.

=== Potential benefits

* You will contribute to the development of a tool designed to support
  investigative journalism practice.
* You will gain early exposure to concepts (PROMPT scoring, boundary objects)
  that may be relevant to evidence evaluation in journalism.
* You will receive a GBP 50 voucher in recognition of your time.
