== Purpose

Month 3 of the 18-month PhD research programme is a formal decision point. The
results of the NUJ user testing (25 participants, 5 sessions) will determine the
project's trajectory. This document defines the criteria, thresholds, and actions
for each possible outcome.

The decision is not binary. Three outcomes are defined: *Continue*, *Modify*, and
*Pivot*. Each outcome is linked to specific quantitative thresholds and
qualitative indicators.

== Decision Framework Overview

[cols="1,1,1,1"]
|===
| Outcome | SUS Score | Task Completion | PROMPT Feedback

| *Continue*
| >= 68
| > 80%
| Positive (mean B1-B10 >= 3.5)

| *Modify*
| 50 - 67
| 60% - 80%
| Mixed (mean B1-B10 = 2.5 - 3.4)

| *Pivot*
| < 50
| < 60%
| Negative (mean B1-B10 < 2.5)
|===

== Outcome 1: Continue

=== Quantitative Thresholds

All of the following must be met:

[cols="1,2,1"]
|===
| Metric | Threshold | Source

| *SUS Score*
| >= 68 (above average usability)
| Feedback Form, Section A

| *Task Completion Rate*
| > 80% of tasks completed across all participants (Tasks 1-7; Task 8 excluded as optional)
| Task Script, completion tracking table

| *PROMPT Comprehension*
| Mean of B1-B5 >= 3.5 (participants understood the scoring system)
| Feedback Form, Section B

| *PROMPT Usefulness*
| Mean of B6-B10 >= 3.5 (participants found scoring useful)
| Feedback Form, Section B

| *Navigation Intuitiveness*
| Mean of C1-C10 >= 3.5 (audience switching and path following were intuitive)
| Feedback Form, Section C

| *Overall Professional Relevance*
| Mean of E1-E5 >= 3.5 (participants see value for journalism)
| Feedback Form, Section E
|===

=== Qualitative Indicators

At least three of the following should be present in debrief conversations:

* Participants spontaneously relate the system to their existing workflow.
* Participants identify specific use cases for PROMPT scoring in their work.
* Participants express interest in audience switching as a communication tool.
* Participants request additional features (indicating engagement, not rejection).
* Participants compare the system favourably to existing evidence management
  tools.

=== Action Plan (If Continue)

. Proceed to Phase 2 development:
  * Zotero browser extension (bidirectional sync)
  * Real-time collaboration (Phoenix Channels/LiveView)
  * Expanded evidence graph (30+ evidence items per investigation)
  * Custom navigation path creation by users
. Schedule Month 6 testing (repeat with 25 NUJ participants, extended feature
  set).
. Prepare interim PhD report with Month 3 findings.
. Publish preliminary usability results at a relevant conference (e.g., Computation
  + Journalism, CSCW, or DIS).

== Outcome 2: Modify

=== Quantitative Thresholds

Any of the following indicate a Modify decision:

[cols="1,2,1"]
|===
| Metric | Threshold | Source

| *SUS Score*
| 50 - 67 (below average but not failing)
| Feedback Form, Section A

| *Task Completion Rate*
| 60% - 80%
| Task Script, completion tracking table

| *PROMPT Comprehension*
| Mean of B1-B5 = 2.5 - 3.4
| Feedback Form, Section B

| *PROMPT Usefulness*
| Mean of B6-B10 = 2.5 - 3.4
| Feedback Form, Section B

| *Navigation Intuitiveness*
| Mean of C1-C10 = 2.5 - 3.4
| Feedback Form, Section C

| *Overall Professional Relevance*
| Mean of E1-E5 = 2.5 - 3.4
| Feedback Form, Section E
|===

=== Qualitative Indicators

The following patterns suggest modification rather than continuation or pivot:

* Participants understand the concepts but struggle with specific interface
  elements.
* Participants express interest in the system's goals but find the execution
  lacking.
* Specific tasks consistently fail while others succeed (localised problems).
* Participants suggest concrete improvements (rather than expressing fundamental
  confusion about purpose).
* The PROMPT concept is understood but the radar chart is not effective.
* Audience switching is valued in principle but the interface is not discoverable.

=== Action Plan (If Modify)

. Identify the specific components that need redesign based on:
  * Which tasks had the lowest completion rates.
  * Which questionnaire sections scored lowest.
  * Common themes in open-ended responses.
. Prioritise modifications using the following severity classification:

[cols="1,3,2"]
|===
| Severity | Description | Timeline

| *Critical*
| Blocks core functionality; participants cannot complete basic tasks
| Fix within 2 weeks

| *Major*
| Significantly impairs usability; participants complete tasks with difficulty
| Fix within 4 weeks

| *Minor*
| Cosmetic or preference-based; does not block task completion
| Fix within 8 weeks
|===

[start=3]
. Implement modifications.
. Conduct a follow-up testing round at Month 4.5 (abbreviated: 10 participants,
  2 sessions) to validate that modifications resolve identified issues.
. If follow-up testing passes Continue thresholds, proceed to Phase 2.
. If follow-up testing still shows Modify or Pivot indicators, escalate to
  supervisory team.

=== Potential Modification Areas

Based on the system architecture, the following areas are most likely to require
modification:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Area | Potential Modifications

| *PROMPT radar chart*
| Replace with bar chart, table, or simplified "traffic light" display; add
  tooltips explaining each dimension; provide an introductory tutorial

| *Audience type selector*
| Increase visual prominence; add before/after comparison view; provide preview
  thumbnails of each audience's view

| *Navigation paths*
| Add progress indicators; improve contextual notes; allow skipping between
  nodes; add a "path overview" map

| *Graph visualisation*
| Simplify initial view; add guided tour overlay; improve node labels and legend;
  reduce visual complexity for small screens

| *Evidence-claim relationships*
| Improve visual distinction between supports/contradicts/contextualises; add
  relationship explanations; provide filtering by relationship type

| *Onboarding*
| Add a brief interactive tutorial for first-time users; include example
  walkthroughs
|===

== Outcome 3: Pivot

=== Quantitative Thresholds

Any of the following indicate a Pivot decision:

[cols="1,2,1"]
|===
| Metric | Threshold | Source

| *SUS Score*
| < 50 (significant usability problems)
| Feedback Form, Section A

| *Task Completion Rate*
| < 60%
| Task Script, completion tracking table

| *PROMPT Comprehension*
| Mean of B1-B5 < 2.5 (participants did not understand the scoring)
| Feedback Form, Section B

| *Navigation Intuitiveness*
| Mean of C1-C10 < 2.5 (audience switching was not intuitive)
| Feedback Form, Section C

| *Overall Professional Relevance*
| Mean of E1-E5 < 2.5 (participants do not see value for journalism)
| Feedback Form, Section E
|===

=== Qualitative Indicators

The following patterns indicate a fundamental problem rather than an interface
issue:

* Participants do not understand the purpose of the system even after using it.
* Participants find the boundary objects concept confusing rather than valuable.
* PROMPT scoring is perceived as arbitrary, paternalistic, or unhelpful.
* Participants express no interest in adopting the system or anything like it.
* The system is compared unfavourably to existing tools without any compensating
  advantages identified.

=== Action Plan (If Pivot)

. Convene an emergency meeting with the PhD supervisory team within 1 week of
  completing data analysis.
. Present the findings and discuss the following pivot options:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Pivot Option | Description

| *Pivot A: Simplify*
| Retain the evidence graph but remove PROMPT scoring and audience switching.
  Focus on a simpler tool for evidence management with graph visualisation only.
  This tests whether the graph structure alone adds value.

| *Pivot B: Refocus audience*
| Retain all three concepts (PROMPT, boundary objects, i-docs) but target a
  different user population (e.g., academic researchers, policy analysts, fact-
  checkers) where epistemological infrastructure may be better received.

| *Pivot C: API-first*
| Abandon the user-facing interface and pivot to a PROMPT scoring API and data
  standard. Other tools (Zotero plugins, CMS integrations, browser extensions)
  would consume the API. This tests whether the concepts are useful even if the
  specific interface is not.

| *Pivot D: Conceptual redesign*
| Retain the theoretical framework but fundamentally redesign the system's
  interaction paradigm based on participant feedback. This may involve a 3-month
  redesign cycle before retesting.
|===

[start=3]
. Select a pivot option with supervisory approval.
. Develop a revised timeline and testing plan for the chosen pivot.
. Schedule the next decision point (typically 3 months after pivot implementation
  begins).

== Data Analysis Plan

=== Quantitative Analysis

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Analysis | Method

| *SUS scoring*
| Calculate per-participant SUS scores using the standard formula (odd items:
  score - 1; even items: 5 - score; multiply sum by 2.5). Report mean, median,
  standard deviation, and range across all 25 participants.

| *Task completion rates*
| Calculate percentage of participants who completed each task (Tasks 1-7).
  Report overall completion rate (total tasks completed / total tasks attempted).
  Note: Task 8 (Zotero) is optional and excluded from the overall rate.

| *Time on task*
| Report mean and median time per task. Identify tasks that consistently exceed
  time estimates (indicating usability problems).

| *Likert-scale items*
| Calculate mean, median, and standard deviation for each section (B, C, D, E).
  Report individual item scores and section means.

| *Correlation analysis*
| Test for correlations between SUS scores and PROMPT comprehension scores
  (Spearman's rho). Test for correlations between career stage and system
  usability ratings.

| *Subgroup analysis*
| Compare scores across participant demographics (career stage, specialism,
  technical confidence) using Kruskal-Wallis H test for non-parametric data with
  small subgroups.
|===

=== Qualitative Analysis

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Analysis | Method

| *Thematic analysis*
| Code open-ended responses and debrief transcripts using reflexive thematic
  analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006). Identify themes related to usability,
  professional relevance, conceptual understanding, and adoption barriers.

| *Affinity diagramming*
| Group observation notes and participant suggestions into clusters to identify
  common pain points and feature requests.

| *Critical incident analysis*
| Identify moments where participants experienced significant difficulty,
  confusion, or insight. Map these to specific interface elements.
|===

=== Reporting

The analysis will produce the following outputs:

. *Internal report* (within 2 weeks of completing all sessions): Summary of
  quantitative metrics and preliminary qualitative themes. This report informs
  the Continue/Modify/Pivot decision.
. *PhD thesis chapter*: Detailed methodology, results, and discussion. Includes
  all quantitative data, thematic analysis, and decision justification.
. *Conference paper* (if Continue): Preliminary findings submitted to a relevant
  venue.
