== Facilitator Instructions

=== Before Each Session

. Verify the staging environment is running and the UK Inflation 2023
  investigation is loaded with complete seed data (7 claims, 30 evidence items,
  10 relationships, 3 navigation paths).
. Confirm screen recording software is ready (with participant consent).
. Have printed copies of the consent form and feedback form available (in-person
  sessions) or digital versions shared via screen (remote sessions).
. Reset any test accounts from previous sessions.
. Note the session start time.

=== During Each Session

* Encourage participants to think aloud throughout all tasks.
* Do not guide participants toward the correct action. If they are stuck for more
  than 2 minutes on a task, offer a single neutral prompt (e.g., "What are you
  looking for?") before providing a hint.
* Record the start and end time of each task.
* Note any errors, hesitations, workarounds, or verbal expressions of confusion
  or satisfaction.
* If a participant cannot complete a task after exhausting hints, mark it as
  incomplete and move to the next task.

=== Participant Briefing Script

Read the following to participants before beginning:

[quote]
____
Thank you for participating in this testing session. You will be using a
prototype system called the Evidence Graph. It is designed to help investigative
journalists navigate, evaluate, and share evidence.

During this session, I will ask you to complete a series of tasks. Please think
aloud as you work -- tell me what you are looking for, what you expect to happen,
and what you notice. There are no right or wrong answers. We are testing the
system, not you.

You may stop at any time. If a task feels impossible, say so and we will move on.

The session will take approximately two hours. Do you have any questions before
we begin?
____

== Task 1: Register and Login

=== Description

Create a new account on the Evidence Graph system and log in.

=== Instructions to Participant

[quote]
____
Please create an account on the system using your email address and a password of
your choice, then log in.
____

=== Expected Outcome

* Participant successfully creates an account with email and password.
* Participant receives confirmation (visual feedback on screen).
* Participant is logged in and sees the main dashboard.

=== Time Estimate

*3 - 5 minutes*

=== Things to Observe

* Does the participant find the registration/login page without difficulty?
* Are the form fields and labels clear?
* Does the participant encounter any validation errors? If so, are the error
  messages understandable?
* How long does the process take from first click to dashboard?
* Does the participant express any confusion about what information is required?

=== Hints (Use Only If Stuck)

. "Can you see a way to create a new account?"
. "Look for a 'Register' or 'Sign Up' link."

---

== Task 2: Browse Dashboard and Find the UK Inflation Investigation

=== Description

Navigate the investigation dashboard and locate the UK Inflation 2023
investigation.

=== Instructions to Participant

[quote]
____
You are now on the dashboard. Please find and open the investigation about UK
inflation in 2023.
____

=== Expected Outcome

* Participant identifies the UK Inflation 2023 investigation from the dashboard
  listing.
* Participant clicks through to the investigation detail view.
* The investigation overview is displayed, showing its title, description, and
  summary of claims and evidence.

=== Time Estimate

*2 - 4 minutes*

=== Things to Observe

* Is the dashboard layout clear? Can the participant distinguish between
  different investigations?
* Does the participant use search, browse, or another method to find the
  investigation?
* How does the participant interpret the investigation summary information
  (number of claims, evidence items, relationships)?
* Does the participant express any expectations about what an "investigation"
  should contain?

=== Hints (Use Only If Stuck)

. "What do you see on this page?"
. "Is there an investigation listed that relates to inflation?"

---

== Task 3: Navigate Claims and Evidence

=== Description

Explore the claims within the UK Inflation investigation, select a claim, and
examine the evidence linked to it.

=== Instructions to Participant

[quote]
____
Within the UK Inflation investigation, please find the main claim about what
drove inflation. Then, look at the evidence that supports or contradicts that
claim.
____

=== Expected Outcome

* Participant locates the primary claim ("Energy prices drove UK inflation higher
  than headline CPI suggests").
* Participant views the list of evidence items linked to the claim.
* Participant distinguishes between supporting evidence, contradicting evidence,
  and contextualising evidence.
* Participant can identify at least one relationship type (supports, contradicts,
  or contextualises).

=== Time Estimate

*5 - 8 minutes*

=== Things to Observe

* Can the participant distinguish between claims and evidence?
* Does the participant understand the relationship types (supports, contradicts,
  contextualises)?
* Does the participant attempt to follow evidence chains (i.e., navigate from one
  claim to linked evidence to another claim)?
* How does the participant interpret confidence levels and relationship weights?
* Does the visual presentation of claims versus evidence cause any confusion?

=== Hints (Use Only If Stuck)

. "Can you see a list of claims for this investigation?"
. "Try clicking on one of the claims to see what evidence is connected."
. "Look for labels like 'supports' or 'contradicts' near the evidence items."

== Task 4: Switch Audience Type and Observe Changes

=== Description

Change the active audience type and observe how the presentation of evidence
changes. This task directly tests the boundary objects concept.

=== Instructions to Participant

[quote]
____
The system allows you to view evidence from different perspectives. Please switch
between the following audience types and tell me what changes you notice:

1. Start as a *Researcher*
2. Switch to *Policymaker*
3. Switch to *Journalist*

For each switch, describe what looks different.
____

=== Expected Outcome

* Participant locates the audience type selector.
* Participant switches between Researcher, Policymaker, and Journalist views.
* Participant observes and articulates at least two of the following changes:
  ** Navigation path order changes (different evidence appears first).
  ** PROMPT score weighting changes (different dimensions are emphasised).
  ** Evidence emphasis changes (different items are highlighted or prioritised).
  ** Contextual framing changes (descriptions or guidance text adapts).

=== Time Estimate

*8 - 12 minutes*

=== Things to Observe

* Can the participant find the audience type selector?
* Does the participant understand _why_ the view changes? (Boundary objects
  concept comprehension.)
* Does the participant notice subtle changes (e.g., re-ordering of evidence) or
  only obvious ones (e.g., different highlighted items)?
* Does the participant express surprise, interest, confusion, or scepticism about
  audience-dependent views?
* Does the participant relate the feature to their own professional practice?
  (e.g., "I could use this to show an editor versus a source.")

=== Hints (Use Only If Stuck)

. "Is there a way to change who you are viewing the evidence as?"
. "Look for a dropdown or selector that mentions audience types."
. "Try comparing what you see as a Researcher versus what you see as a
   Policymaker."

== Task 5: View Evidence Graph Visualisation

=== Description

Open and interact with the D3.js evidence graph visualisation.

=== Instructions to Participant

[quote]
____
Please find and open the graph view of this investigation. Once you can see the
graph, try the following:

1. Identify a claim node and an evidence node.
2. Find a relationship between them.
3. Try zooming in or out.
4. Click on a node to see its details.
____

=== Expected Outcome

* Participant opens the graph visualisation view.
* Participant can distinguish between claim nodes and evidence nodes (by colour,
  shape, or label).
* Participant identifies at least one edge (relationship) and its type.
* Participant successfully zooms and/or pans.
* Participant clicks a node and views its detail panel (including PROMPT scores
  if available).

=== Time Estimate

*5 - 8 minutes*

=== Things to Observe

* Is the graph rendering performant? Any lag or visual glitches?
* Can the participant read node labels at the default zoom level?
* Does the participant intuitively try to interact with the graph (drag, click,
  zoom) or wait for instructions?
* Does the participant understand what the graph is representing?
* Does the participant find the graph useful or overwhelming?
* Does the participant relate the graph to the claims and evidence they explored
  in Task 3?

=== Hints (Use Only If Stuck)

. "Is there a 'Graph' or 'Visualise' option in the investigation view?"
. "Try scrolling to zoom, or clicking and dragging to move around."
. "The coloured circles represent claims and evidence. Try clicking one."

== Task 6: Examine PROMPT Scores on the Radar Chart

=== Description

Locate and interpret PROMPT scores displayed as a radar chart for a specific
piece of evidence.

=== Instructions to Participant

[quote]
____
Please find the PROMPT scores for the ONS CPI Data evidence item. You should see
a radar chart showing six dimensions. Please tell me:

1. Which dimension scores highest?
2. Which dimension scores lowest?
3. What do you think these scores mean?
4. How useful would this information be in your work?
____

=== Expected Outcome

* Participant locates the PROMPT radar chart for the ONS CPI Data item.
* Participant correctly identifies the highest-scoring dimension(s) (Provenance:
  100, Replicability: 100, Publication: 100).
* Participant correctly identifies the lowest-scoring dimension(s) (Objective:
  95, Methodology: 95, Transparency: 95 -- all are high for this item).
* Participant articulates an understanding of what the dimensions mean (even if
  approximate).
* Participant offers an opinion on professional usefulness.

=== Time Estimate

*5 - 8 minutes*

=== Things to Observe

* Can the participant find the PROMPT scores without assistance?
* Does the participant understand the radar chart format? (Six axes, 0-100
  scale.)
* Can the participant read the dimension labels on the radar chart?
* Does the participant understand the difference between individual dimension
  scores and the overall score?
* Does the participant compare PROMPT scores across different evidence items
  (unprompted)?
* Does the participant express opinions about the scoring criteria? (e.g., "I
  would weight Provenance higher" or "What counts as Transparency?")
* Does the "nutrition label" metaphor resonate?

=== Hints (Use Only If Stuck)

. "Look for a chart with six axes, like a spider web or radar."
. "Each axis represents a quality dimension. The further from the centre, the
   higher the score."
. "Try looking at the detail panel for the ONS CPI Data evidence item."

== Task 7: Follow a Navigation Path as a Specific Audience

=== Description

Select an audience-specific navigation path and follow it through the evidence
graph from start to finish.

=== Instructions to Participant

[quote]
____
Please select the *Policymaker* audience type and follow the suggested navigation
path from beginning to end. As you move through each step, please tell me:

1. What you see at each step.
2. Whether the order makes sense for a policymaker.
3. Whether you would change anything about the path.
____

=== Expected Outcome

* Participant selects the Policymaker audience type.
* Participant identifies and begins the Policymaker navigation path.
* Participant navigates through each path node in sequence (expected order:
  official statistics first, then authoritative claims, then recommendations).
* Participant articulates whether the ordering is appropriate for a policymaker
  audience.
* Participant completes the path or explains why they stopped.

=== Time Estimate

*8 - 12 minutes*

=== Things to Observe

* Does the participant understand what a "navigation path" is?
* Does the participant find it natural to follow a prescribed sequence, or do
  they want to deviate?
* Does the participant understand the contextual notes at each path node (the
  "why am I seeing this now?" text)?
* Does the participant agree that the path ordering suits a policymaker?
* Does the participant spontaneously compare this path to how they would navigate
  the evidence independently?
* Does the participant suggest improvements to the path content or ordering?

=== Hints (Use Only If Stuck)

. "Look for a 'Paths' or 'Navigation' section within the investigation."
. "There should be a path labelled for policymakers. Try selecting it."
. "Follow the numbered steps in order, reading the context provided at each
   step."

== Task 8: Import Evidence from Zotero (Optional)

=== Description

Import a reference from Zotero into the investigation. This task is optional and
is only attempted if the participant uses Zotero or is willing to try.

=== Instructions to Participant

[quote]
____
If you use Zotero for reference management, or are willing to try, please attempt
to import a reference into the UK Inflation investigation. A sample Zotero JSON
file is provided.

If you do not use Zotero, you may skip this task.
____

=== Expected Outcome

* Participant locates the import functionality.
* Participant either uploads the sample JSON file or enters reference details
  manually.
* The imported reference appears in the investigation's evidence list.
* PROMPT scores are either auto-populated (partially) or left blank for manual
  entry.

=== Time Estimate

*5 - 10 minutes* (if attempted)

=== Things to Observe

* Does the participant use Zotero in their current workflow?
* Is the import process intuitive for someone unfamiliar with JSON?
* Does the participant understand how imported references relate to the evidence
  graph?
* Does the participant expect bi-directional sync (changes in Zotero reflected in
  the Evidence Graph and vice versa)?
* What file formats does the participant expect to be able to import (BibTeX,
  RIS, CSV, etc.)?

=== Hints (Use Only If Stuck)

. "Look for an 'Import' or 'Add Evidence' button."
. "There is a sample file provided. Try uploading it."
. "If the import button is not obvious, it may be in a menu or toolbar."

== Post-Task Debrief

After completing all tasks (or as many as time permits), proceed to the
link:feedback-form.adoc[Feedback Form] questionnaire, followed by a semi-structured
debrief conversation.

=== Debrief Questions

Ask the following questions during the 20-minute debrief:

. *Overall impression*: "What is your overall impression of the system?"
. *Professional relevance*: "Can you see yourself using a system like this in
  your investigative work? Why or why not?"
. *PROMPT scoring*: "Did the six-dimension scoring make sense to you? Would you
  trust scores generated this way?"
. *Audience switching*: "What did you think about seeing the same evidence from
  different perspectives? Is that useful?"
. *Navigation paths*: "Did the suggested paths add value, or would you prefer to
  explore freely?"
. *Missing features*: "What is missing that would make this useful for your
  work?"
. *Comparison*: "How does this compare to tools you currently use for managing
  evidence or sources?"
. *Adoption barriers*: "What would prevent you or your colleagues from adopting a
  system like this?"
